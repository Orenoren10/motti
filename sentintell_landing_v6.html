How it works

Four steps to a decision you can defend

You define the economic stakes. Sentintell calculates whether the forecast justifies acting.

01

Define the decision

Region, variable, threshold, forecast day — plus your cost of acting (C) and loss if you don't (L).

02

Ensemble forecast

A 31-member GEFS ensemble computes the calibrated probability your threshold is crossed.

03

Cost–loss analysis

The Murphy–Richardson framework weighs that probability against your C/L ratio for expected value.

04

A decision, with evidence

One call — ACT or DO NOT ACT — its dollar value, and the historical record that stands behind it.

High-value agriculture

One frost can erase a season. The decision is whether to defend it.

For vineyards, orchards and other high-value perennial crops, protection is cheap and known; a lost crop is catastrophic. That asymmetry is exactly where a calibrated forecast is worth the most — and what Sentintell quantifies.

  • Heat stress: pre-cool or shade ahead of an extreme
  • Harvest window: act on the dry-day signal

The science

A method with a name — and scientists behind it

  • Established decision theory. The Murphy–Richardson cost–loss framework has been used for decades to decide when a forecast justifies protective action. Sentintell automates it for your exact numbers.
  • Calibrated ensemble probabilities. Raw GEFS output is post-processed with isotonic calibration, so a stated 80% means 80% in the historical record — not a model artefact.
  • Verification you can inspect. Reliability tables, historical analogs and threshold-continuity checks ship with every result — and the engine flags when a case sits outside what history supports.
